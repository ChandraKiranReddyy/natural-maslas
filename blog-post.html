The Real Difference Between Garam Masala and Curry Powder

Outside India, "curry powder" and "garam masala" get used as if they're the same jar with two different labels. They're not, and the mix-up changes more about a dish than most recipes let on.

Curry powder is a British-era invention — a single, shelf-stable blend meant to stand in for the dozens of masalas an Indian kitchen would mix fresh for different dishes. It almost always includes turmeric (for the colour), which is why curry powder is yellow and garam masala is brown.

Garam masala means "warm spice mix." It's built from spices considered warming in Ayurvedic terms — cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, black pepper, cumin — and it's added at the end of cooking, not the start, so the aroma stays sharp instead of cooking off.

Swap one for the other and you'll still get "a curry," but you'll lose the thing that made the original dish specific. That's the whole reason we keep them as separate SKUs instead of one all-purpose blend.

If you're only going to keep one in the cabinet, keep garam masala — it's the one that's actually hard to replicate from loose spices at home, because the ratio matters more than any single ingredient.
